ABSTRACT 

Long chain-of-thought (CoT) traces impose substantial output-token costs. Under constrained budgets, compression must preserve answer-critical information, making boundary placement central. Token-level and fixed-length boundaries can fragment coherent spans such as phrases, formulas, and local derivations, whereas step-level boundaries can bind content requiring different compres-sion actions. We introduce SynLat, a text–latent CoT framework that aligns compression boundaries with syntactic structure through non-overlapping Syntax-Aligned Units (SAUs). An answer-conditioned Teacher constructs progressive KEEP/LATENT targets for a single compression-conditioned Student, which generates mixed reasoning from only the question and requested compression level at infer-ence. Across two Qwen3 Student scales, Standard- and Long-CoT groups, and three compression levels, SynLat matches or exceeds the strongest evaluated baseline in all 12 task-group aggregates and strictly leads in 11 under the reported achieved-CR selection pro-tocol. Overall gains reach 3.6/2.6 points at MEDIUM and 7.0/5.5 points at HIGH for Qwen3-8B/14B, with larger advantages under stronger compression, particularly on Long-CoT groups.

1. INTRODUCTION 

Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting makes intermediate reasoning explicit [1], but long traces increase output-token cost. Explicit compression methods include token pruning [2], chunk compres-sion [3], step-entropy filtering [4], and formula preservation [5]. At aggressive ratios, however, deleting or condensing text can reduce logical fidelity and fragment symbolic expressions [5]. Latent compression instead moves reasoning into non-text car-riers. COCONUT propagates hidden states as continuous reasoning states [6], whereas CODI distills explicit CoT into continuous space [7]. With the trace fully replaced, latent states must carry complete, precise derivations without textual anchors. Moreover, SIM-CoT re-ports that scaling implicit reasoning can destabilize training as latent representations become homogeneous and lose semantic diversity [8]. Hybrid methods therefore retain selected text: Token Assorted mixes it with discrete latent symbols [9], while SPOT replaces se-lected spans with pause states [10]. Compression then becomes a representation-fidelity allocation problem: which content remains textual and which becomes latent. Abstract 

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning improves the problem-solving ability of large language models, but long reason-ing traces impose substantial output-token costs. Under con-strained output budgets, CoT compression must shorten rea-soning while preserving answer-critical information, mak-ing compression-boundary placement critical: token-level and fixed-length boundaries can fragment semantically coher-ent spans such as phrases, formulas, and local derivations, whereas step-level boundaries can bind content that requires different compression actions. We introduce CoL-STaR, a text–latent CoT compression framework that aligns compres-sion boundaries with syntactic structure to construct Syntax-Aligned Units (SAUs). During offline target construction, CoL-STaR uses reference answers to derive progressive text– latent assignments over SAUs and trains a single model to reproduce this allocation; at inference, this model generates controllable mixed reasoning using only the question and re-quested compression ratio. Across two model scales, short and long reasoning task groups, and three compression ratios, CoL-STaR establishes a new state of the art in the accuracy– compression trade-off across all 12 evaluated settings, with outright gains in 11 of them. Its advantage is largest under ag-gressive compression and on tasks requiring longer reasoning traces.

T (r) = ( V, E ) whose leaves are the tokens w1, . . . , w n and whose internal nodes v ∈ V each cover a continuous token span cv = wa(v): b(v) = ( wa(v), . . . , w b(v)).Figure 3 illustrates this with the sentence “Natalia sold

2. RELATION TO PRIOR WORK Explicit CoT and visible compression. Chain-of-thought prompt-ing exposes intermediate reasoning [1], whose growing traces mo-tivate compression within natural language. TokenSkip prunes to-kens [2]; R1-Compress condenses chunks [3]; step-entropy filtering selects steps by entropy [4]; and Extra-CoT preserves formulas at aggressive ratios [5]. These methods remain within visible text and delete or rewrite predefined units, motivating representations beyond text. 

Latent and hybrid text–latent reasoning. Latent methods move reasoning beyond ordinary text. COCONUT propagates continu-ous hidden states [6], while CODI distills explicit CoT into continu-ous space [7]. SIM-CoT reports that scaling implicit reasoning can destabilize training and homogenize latent states [8]. DLR learns a discrete vocabulary from rendered CoT features [12]. Hybrid meth-ods therefore retain textual anchors: Token Assorted mixes text with discrete symbols [9], SPOT replaces spans with pause states [10], Selective Latent Thinking gates latent spans by confidence [13], and A*-Thought-V2 geometrically selects step-level continuous states [14]. Prior summarization work uses constituency parses to define deletable spans [15]. SynLat differs by flattening trace-specific syn-tax into ordered, non-overlapping SAUs and learning compression-conditioned text–latent allocation over a shared discrete codebook.

5. RESULTS Main results. Under the reported achieved-CR selection protocol, Table 1 shows aggregate accuracy gains for SynLat at MEDIUM and HIGH across both Student scales, increasing with compression. At MEDIUM, it exceeds the strongest evaluated baseline overall by 3.6 and 2.6 percentage points for Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B, re-spectively. At HIGH, the margins widen to 7.0 and 5.5 points. For Qwen3-8B at HIGH, the margin is 4.7 points on Standard-CoT and 8.5 points on Long-CoT. Baselines outperform SynLat on MATH-500 for Qwen3-8B at LOW, ProofWriter for Qwen3-14B at LOW, and StrategyQA for Qwen3-14B at MEDIUM. 

Compression-unit ablation. At approximately 44% achieved CR, Table 2 shows that SAUs retain 94.0% of NONE accuracy. Fixed 5-token windows trail by 2.6 points, showing that a similar length cap is insufficient; guarded windows still trail by 1.8 points, so sim-ple boundary protection does not recover syntax-aligned allocation. Steps trail by 4.4 points despite low split rates, indicating that coarse units hinder selective compression. SAUs therefore best balance for-mula preservation and granularity among the tested unit definitions for mixed text–latent compression. Token Deletion is not a pure boundary control because it also changes the compressed represen-tation. 

Planning and training ablations. On fixed SAUs, answer-conditioned planning retains 94.0%/89.3% of NONE accuracy at MEDIUM/HIGH, versus 92.0%/86.2% for the strongest answer-free alternatives. Removing action-plan supervision reduces mean accuracy across the three compressed levels from 73.1 to 70.6, supporting the planning-and-transfer design. 

6. CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we revisit CoT compression as a problem of allo-cating limited representation fidelity. We propose SynLat, a text– latent compression framework that bases compression decisions on Syntax-Aligned Units, allowing local reasoning content to remain in text or be converted into latent representations according to its role. Experiments show that, under matched output budgets, Syn-Lat achieves higher final-answer accuracy than representative com-pression methods, with particularly large gains on tasks that require longer reasoning traces. These results indicate that CoT compression is not only about reducing visible tokens, but also about maintaining a better match between reasoning structure and answer-relevant in-formation during compression. 7.
