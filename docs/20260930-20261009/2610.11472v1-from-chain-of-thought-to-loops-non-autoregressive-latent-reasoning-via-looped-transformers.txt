# ABSTRACT 

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning often improves language-model performance by giving models additional computation before answering. However, explicit CoT expresses this computation as a sequence of autoregressively generated to-kens. Latent reasoning replaces these tokens with compact continuous states, but most autoregressive latent-reasoning methods retain a left-to-right dependency among latent vectors. We introduce LLoCoT: a looped latent-reasoning frame-work that replaces left-to-right latent generation with iterative refinement of a compact latent workspace. A shared transformer is reapplied for a small number of refinement iterations, jointly updating the latent slots based on the prompt and the evolving workspace state. Using the refined state, a probabilistic head predicts a distribution from which latent tokens are sampled in parallel and used to con-dition an autoregressive decoder for answer generation. Training uses continuous representations derived from explicit CoT together with a final-answer prediction loss and likelihood-based supervision of the latent states. Across HumanEval and MBPP, LLoCoT achieves the highest mean among the evaluated methods, per-forming on par in accuracy with Reasoning SFT, our explicit-CoT baseline, while outperforming the base model, answer-only SFT and NF-CoT. Relative to Rea-soning SFT, LLoCoT reduces time to the first answer token by approximately 

36 × and reasoning-phase latency by approximately 42 ×, while increasing end-to-end throughput by 9.2% . This design replaces serial thought generation with parallel latent-slot refinement while retaining probabilistic latent modeling and autoregressive answer decoding. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Test-time computation has become an important means of improving the reasoning capabilities of language models. Rather than producing an answer immediately, a model can devote additional computation to decomposing a problem, developing intermediate conclusions, and revising its solu-tion. Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning realizes this process by generating an explicit sequence of reasoning tokens before the final answer. However, CoT ties reasoning computation to autoregres-sive language generation, as every intermediate thought must be expressed in natural language and generated after all preceding tokens. Increasing the amount of reasoning therefore requires extend-ing a strictly sequential trace, even when the intermediate computation is needed only internally and does not need to be communicated to the user. Continuous latent reasoning relaxes the requirement that intermediate computation be represented as text. A reasoning trace can be encoded into a compact collection of continuous vectors that act as learned aggregators of information from the complete trace. These vectors need not correspond to individual words, sentences, or human-interpretable steps. However, most existing latent-reasoning approaches generate their latent states autoregressively. LLoCoT , our architecture, is motivated by the intuition that latent thoughts summarizing a complete reasoning trace need not follow a fixed

Instead of forming a sequence that is extended from left to right, they can constitute a shared workspace that is repeatedly refined through parameter-shared computation, in the spirit of TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025). We instantiate this idea using continuous latent targets derived from NF-CoT (Tu et al., 2026). LLo-CoT initializes a fixed set of learned hidden-state slots and repeatedly refines them with a shared transformer. At each non-final iteration, the predicted latent means are projected back into the workspace to guide the next refinement step. After the final iteration, all latent thoughts are sam-pled jointly from the predicted Gaussian distribution and used to condition autoregressive answer generation. The model is trained with a likelihood objective on the final latent predictions, aux-iliary supervision that aligns intermediate predictions with the same complete-trace target, and a cross-entropy objective for answer generation. A frozen VAE and a conditional flow construct these continuous training targets, but neither is required at inference time. Across several coding benchmarks, we find that LLoCoT performs on par with reasoning SFT while outperforming the base model, answer-only supervised fine-tuning, and NF-CoT on aggregate. In particular, LLoCoT consistently improves over NF-CoT, indicating that iterative refinement of a shared latent space provides an effective alternative to autoregressive latent-thought generation. In terms of efficiency, LLoCoT substantially shortens the reasoning phase relative to both textual chain-of-thought and autoregressive latent reasoning. Its parallelizable refinement process brings the time to the first answer token close to that of direct answer generation and yields the lowest end-to-end latency among the evaluated methods. We summarize our contributions as follows: • We introduce LLoCoT, an architecture that replaces autoregressive latent-thought genera-tion with recurrent refinement of a fixed-size latent workspace. • We show that LLoCoT performs on par with Reasoning SFT while outperforming the base model, answer-only supervised fine-tuning and NF-CoT. • We show that LLoCoT substantially reduces reasoning latency relative to both textual and autoregressive latent reasoning, yielding the lowest end-to-end latency among the evaluated methods.

Latent reasoning. Chain-of-thought prompting improves language-model reasoning by allocating additional computation through intermediate textual tokens (Wei et al., 2023). Latent-reasoning methods move this computation into continuous hidden states, avoiding the requirement that ev-ery intermediate step be verbalized. Coconut (Hao et al., 2026) introduced an early instance of this paradigm by feeding a model’s hidden state directly back as its next input embedding. Sub-sequent approaches have explored alternative generative models for continuous thoughts. LaDiR (Kang et al., 2026), for example, encodes reasoning traces with a variational autoencoder (VAE) and iteratively denoises blocks of latent thoughts using diffusion, replacing left-to-right generation with non-autoregressive refinement. Most closely related to our work is NF-CoT (Tu et al., 2026), which encodes explicit reasoning traces into compact VAE representations and uses normalizing flows to construct a tractable distribution over continuous thoughts. NF-CoT preserves probabilistic sampling and likelihood-based training, but generates its latent thoughts autoregressively. We build on its VAE- and flow-based target construction while replacing sequential latent generation with the joint, iterative refinement of a fixed latent workspace. 

Looped transformers. Looped transformers repeatedly apply a shared block to an evolving hid-den state, decoupling computational depth from parameter count. Prior empirical work has shown that this recurrence can support iterative state manipulation and the execution of algorithmic proce-dures (Saunshi et al., 2025; Kohli et al., 2026). More recently, HRM (Wang et al., 2025) and TRM (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025) demonstrated that recurrent refinement can also solve highly structured reasoning problems such as Sudoku, maze planning, and ARC-AGI with relatively small models. Although these results are obtained in constrained domains, they suggest that repeatedly updating a latent state provides a useful inductive bias for multi-step computation. Along a complementary line of work, looped architectures have also begun to scale to general-purpose language modeling (Zhu et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026).

Let q denote an input prompt, d an explicit reasoning trace available during training, and y the corresponding final answer. At inference, the model receives only q. LLoCoT replaces left-to-right latent-thought generation with iterative refinement of a fixed set of learned workspace slots. The same transformer is reapplied for R refinement iterations, and the resulting latent thoughts condition an autoregressive answer decoder. The overall computation is illustrated in Figure 1. The model maintains K hidden-state slots of width dh, where dh is the hidden dimension of the transformer backbone. It predicts K final latent thoughts of width D and uses them to condition answer generation. The table clearly shows that LLoCoT substantially reduces the latency of the reasoning phase. Its time to first answer token is only 0.09 seconds, compared with 3.17 seconds for Reasoning SFT and 2.01 seconds for NF-CoT, making it the fastest reasoning method and bringing its startup latency close to that of the base model, which performs no explicit reasoning. Thus, LLoCoT’s wall-clock advantage arises primarily from replacing serial latent generation with more parallelizable refinement, rather than from reducing total arithmetic computation. 

# 5 LIMITATIONS AND CONCLUSIONS 

5.1 LIMITATIONS 

Our study has several limitations. First, all experiments use a 1.7B-parameter backbone, so it re-mains unclear whether the observed benefits extend to larger models. Second, the evaluation is limited to code-generation tasks and does not establish generalization to other reasoning domains. Third, LLoCoT uses more peak memory than Reasoning SFT and more analytical FLOPs than the other evaluated reasoning methods; its speedup therefore results from replacing serial reasoning with more parallelizable computation rather than reducing total computational cost. Finally, as with many latent-reasoning approaches, the workspace size and refinement depth must be selected in advance rather than adapted dynamically to the difficulty of each input. 5.2 CONCLUSIONS 

We introduce LLoCoT, a latent-reasoning architecture that replaces autoregressive latent-thought generation with recurrent refinement of a fixed-size latent state. LLoCoT repeatedly applies a shared 8transformer, feeds intermediate latent predictions back into subsequent refinement iterations, and jointly generates the final latent thoughts used to condition answer decoding. This design combines complete-trace continuous targets with a more parallelizable reasoning process. Across four code-generation benchmarks, LLoCoT performs on par with Reasoning SFT while outperforming the base model, answer-only supervised fine-tuning, and NF-CoT on aggregate. It also obtains higher point estimates than NF-CoT on every evaluated benchmark. Most notably, LLoCoT substantially shortens the reasoning phase relative to both textual chain-of-thought and autoregressive latent rea-soning, resulting in the lowest measured end-to-end latency. These results suggest that recurrent latent-workspace refinement is a promising approach for preserving the benefits of learned latent reasoning while reducing its sequential inference bottleneck. A.1 TARGET VAE The continuous targets were produced with a transformer-based VAE implementation of the NF-CoT target interface. Both the encoder and decoder use Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B-Base , whose hidden width is 2048 . The encoder is causal, uses K = 64 learned query slots, and predicts a D = 2560 -dimensional latent vector for each slot. The VAE is not prompt-conditioned and does not use block segmentation. The VAE objective uses β = 0 .01 , with posterior log-variances clipped to [−10 , 10] . VAE training uses AdamW with learning rate 10 −5 and weight decay 0.01 , a per-GPU batch size of 4, gradient accumulation of 4, two GPUs, and an effective batch size of 32 . Training uses bfloat16 precision, gradient checkpointing, a maximum sequence length of 3119 , seed 0, and 180 ,030 optimizer steps. Latent extraction is deterministic and stores posterior means. These cached posterior means con-stitute the fixed VAE-derived target representation. The VAE encoder remains frozen during all subsequent LLoCoT training. The VAE decoder is not used during LLoCoT training or inference. During LLoCoT training, Gaussian dequantization noise with standard deviation 0.3 is added to the cached representation before target-space flow transformation. A.2 LL OCOT A RCHITECTURE 

The answer model is Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B-Base , with hidden width dh = 2048 , 28 transformer layers, 16 attention heads, and 8 key–value heads. LLoCoT uses K = 64 loop hidden-state slots, fi-nal latent thoughts of width D = 2560 , and R = 2 refinement loops. The full transformer backbone is reapplied at every loop using causal attention. The learned initial loop hidden states are initialized from N (0 , 0.02 2), and stochastic seed noise is disabled. The latent head is a shared diagonal Gaussian head applied independently to each slot. It maps each hidden-state vector to 2D outputs, corresponding to the mean and scale of one final latent thought. The head is zero-initialized. Means and log-scales use a soft-clipped transformation with clipping value 4.0, and scales are computed with softplus followed by a minimum value of 10 −5.Mean feedback is applied after every non-final loop. The intermediate predicted mean is projected into the hidden dimension with 

LayerNorm( D) → Linear( D, d h) → GELU → Linear( dh, d h),

and added to the next loop hidden states. This projection is shared with the projection used to condition answer decoding. The intermediate Gaussian objective has weight 0.25 and is applied only to non-final loops, with the target treated as stop-gradient. Prompt dropout is applied with probability 0.1.The target-space flow contains five causal affine blocks, two conditioner layers per block, a con-ditioner hidden size of 2048 , and 32 attention heads. The latent-slot ordering alternates between identity and reversal across blocks. The flow is trained as a target-space adapter during LLoCoT training and is not executed during inference. A.3 PARAMETER STATUS AND TRAINING STAGES 

The VAE encoder is pretrained separately and remains frozen during all LLoCoT stages. The target-space flow is not separately pretrained. Each affine block is initialized to the identity transformation by zero-initializing its shift and log-scale output projection. The flow is then optimized jointly with the other trainable LLoCoT modules during both LLoCoT training stages. Stage 1 freezes the Qwen transformer backbone. The loop-specific modules, latent Gaussian head, target-space flow, latent projection, and learned initial hidden states are trained. Stage 2 is initialized from the Stage 1 checkpoint and unfreezes the Qwen backbone. The target-space flow and loop-specific modules remain trainable unless explicitly disabled by the experiment configuration. 11 The VAE-derived representation is treated as fixed with respect to the VAE parameters. B.5 INFERENCE -E FFICIENCY PROTOCOL 

Efficiency measurements use 128 fixed coding prompts, batch size one, a single NVIDIA H100 80GB HBM3 GPU, and bfloat16. Each prompt is measured three times after ten warm-up runs, yielding 384 measured records per endpoint. The efficiency answer-length limit is 2048 tokens and is separate from the accuracy-generation cap. Warm-up, tokenization, sampling, network, and benchmark-evaluation time are excluded. For Reasoning SFT, the reasoning phase ends at the first </think> boundary. For NF-CoT and LLoCoT, the corresponding phase is serial latent generation or looped latent refinement. Condition-ing or latent selection and answer decoding are measured separately when applicable. Time to first answer token is measured from the start of model execution to the first answer token. End-to-end latency includes the measured reasoning, conditioning, and answer-generation phases. Peak allocated memory is recorded during each measured run. Analytical FLOPs are deterministic estimates. Forward-pass counts are recorded directly by the evaluation counter and are averaged over the mea-sured prompt–run records; consequently, they may be non-integer.
