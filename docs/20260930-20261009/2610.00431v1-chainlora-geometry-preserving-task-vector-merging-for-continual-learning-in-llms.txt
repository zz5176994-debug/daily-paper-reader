ABSTRACT 

Continual parameter-efficient fine-tuning for large language models (LLMs) must balance retention of previously acquired knowledge, adaptation to new tasks, and strict parameter budgets. We present ChainLoRA , a replay-free continual merging framework built on chain-updated task-vector geometry. From a parameter-merging perspective, we formulate a geometric view of forgetting through a measurable interaction between task updates, separating directional overlap from coefficient coupling. Building on this view, ChainLoRA combines chain-updated training with post-stream adaptive SVD merging. During training, initialization and a one-sided orthogonality proxy use only the last carrier, keeping their historical-state footprint and regularization overhead constant as the task stream grows. At merging time, Adaptive SVD extracts a shared carrier and aligns it to the latest task through Procrustes adaptation. Our theoretical analysis shows that Procrustes adaptation fa-cilitates geometric approximate separation of shared and task-specific components. The one-sided proxy further bounds inter-task interference. An effective-rank penalty additionally promotes efficient utilization of the task subspace during continual learning. Experiments show that ChainLoRA achieves state-of-the-art performance among the evaluated replay-free methods on the Large and SuperNI benchmarks, while remaining competitive on Standard CL and attaining almost the closest average scores to the evaluated replay-based method across all three benchmarks.

Foundation models, e.g. large language models (LLMs), have shown remarkable generalization and reasoning abilities across diverse domains. When they are subsequently finetuned on a stream of domains (Karanikolas et al., 2023), later updates often overwrite earlier ones— catastrophic forgetting (McCloskey & Cohen, 1989; Liu et al., 2024). Continual learning (Shi et al., 2025) studies how to acquire new knowledge without this collapse. Regularization methods (Kirk-patrick et al., 2017) keep parameters close to a previous solution, but can hinder adaptation to new tasks. Replay methods (Rebuffi et al., 2017) revisit old data, which is often unavailable or costly during post-training. Ar-chitectural isolation (Tong et al., 2025) assigns tasks to separate modules, at the price of extra parameters and weaker sharing (Verma et al., 2021), which may further limit generalization across tasks. These limits raise a concrete question: Can a CL method avoid catastrophic forgetting without compromising cross-task generalization or relying on data replay?

The second challenge is efficient use of limited adapta-tion capacity as Fig. 3. OLoRA enforcing orthogonality between entire task updates can be overly restrictive, as different tasks often share transferable knowledge. In the extreme, even splitting the same task into two sequen-tial stages would force their updates into separate direc-tions, unnecessarily consuming additional parameter space. Such over-separation reduces the capacity available for long-term adaptation as more tasks arrive. Architectural isolation faces a similar limitation: allocating new parame-ters to different tasks primarily postpones capacity satura-tion through model expansion, rather than improving the reuse of existing capacity. Meanwhile, isolating tasks in separate modules may also limit cross-task generalization. We treat that question as a problem of task-vector geometry rather than of allocating extra modules. Building upon orthogonalization, we further decompose each task update into a shared component and a task-specific component, instead of enforcing orthogonality between entire task vectors. We encourage the task-specific components to be orthogonal to the shared component and to one another, allowing transferable knowledge to occupy shared directions while isolating task-specific variations. This structured decomposition preserves the benefits of orthogonality without unnecessarily separating knowledge that can be reused across tasks. 2A well-adopted observation is that a low-dimensional update can match full-parameter fine-tuning (Wang et al., 2023). Write W = W0 + ∆ W with W0 frozen and ∆W = BA , so CL knowledge lives in a small additive factor. In a continual task stream, that factor is decomposed into tasks and still couples in two ways: the induced directions of different tasks, and their coefficients can activate those. We specifically standardize each update by a thin QR factorization, taking the row-orthonormal factor as the task vector (carrier) and the matching coefficients as magnitude. The method then acts at two timescales, because the objects available during training and after the stream are not the same. During the stream, the post-hoc merge carrier is unavailable: only the last task’s carrier can be copied forward. Chain-updated initialization starts the next task from the latest. A one-sided proxy loss encourages disentanglement of common knowledge from that of the task-specific knowledge, while a smoothed group penalty on magnitude columns encourages lower-rank updates, preserving adaptation capacity for future tasks and mitigating potential parameter-space crowding. After the stream, Adaptive SVD merging estimates a rank-r shared carrier, aligns to the latest task by Procrustes adaptation, and damps residuals according to their distance from it. Finally, the merged update is absorbed into a single adapter for inference. Highlights of our contribution are: 

(1) We cast catastrophic forgetting as an operational geometric quantity in merging: each update indicates a carrier with magnitude, naive merging couples them through inner products, and orthogo-nality is sufficient for weight-space disentanglement. (2) On this geometry, we develop ChainLoRA, combining chain-updated training with a post-stream Adaptive SVD merge. The online penalty and initialization use only the last carrier, so historical cost stays constant in the CL stream. (3) We provide theoretical analysis showing that Procrustes adaptation facilitates geometric approximate sep-aration of shared and task-specific components, while the one-sided proxy further bounds inter-task interference. (4) Experiments indicate strong replay-free performance. On the long-horizon Large stream, ChainLoRA is the strongest among published replay-free LoRA and merging baselines we compare; on shorter Standard CL it approaches replay-augmented methods such as SAPT-LoRA. 

2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 CONTINUAL LEARNING AND CATASTROPHIC FORGETTING 

Continual learning studies how a model can learn a sequence of tasks while mitigating catastrophic forgetting . Existing methods fall into three families. Regularization-based methods (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017; Lopez-Paz & Ranzato, 2017; Farajtabar et al., 2020) constrain updates via importance-weighted penalties to preserve past knowledge, but strong constraints can hinder plasticity. Replay-based methods (Rebuffi et al., 2017; Rolnick et al., 2019; Buzzega et al., 2020; de Masson D’Autume et al., 2019) mix old or pseudo samples with new data; for foundation-model post-training, storing historical data is often infeasible due to privacy or licensing, and generative replay adds overhead. 

Architecture-based (structural isolation) methods (Aljundi et al., 2017; Mallya & Lazebnik, 2018; Wang et al., 2022b; Verma et al., 2021) allocate a separate module per task, reducing interference at the cost of more parameters and less sharing. Our work keeps the isolation but realizes it geometrically: task share a carrier when they can, and otherwise remain as residuals inside one LoRA budget. Within LoRA-based continual learning, O-LoRA enforces orthogonality in low-rank updates (Wang et al., 2023), InfLoRA introduces interference-aware subspace control (Liang & Li, 2024), SAPT-LoRA augments replay via synthetic historical samples (Zhao et al., 2024), and recent variants such as LoRM (Salami et al., 2025), CorDA (Yang et al., 2024), and MagMax (Marczak et al., 2024) explore different constraints on low-rank adaptation. They typically optimize per-task adaptation rather than a unified geometry for continual merging. 2.2 LOW -R ANK ADAPTATION AND PARAMETER -E FFICIENT FINE -T UNING 

Large language models can be adapted with far fewer trainable parameters than the full network. LoRA Hu et al. (2022) parameterizes the update as ∆W = BA (with frozen W0 and rank r ≪ d), consistent with the observation that fine-tuning often has low intrinsic dimension Li et al. (2018). These methods reduce cost but typically use a single adapter or LoRA per model (or per task), and do not specify a shared-trunk / residual geometry for composing a sequence of updates. 3Parallel to PEFT, recent work studies merging independently trained adapters without original data (Ilharco et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Yadav et al., 2023; Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2023; Wortsman et al., 2022), including LoRA-LEGO’s structured recombination (Zhao et al., 2025), KnOTS’s SVD-oriented task arithmetic (Stoica et al., 2025), and OPCM for streaming updates (Tang et al., 2025). Most existing formulations are not specifically designed for asymmetric LoRA factor roles under continual adaptation, which motivates our chain-updated geometry-aware merging design.

Base Models. We evaluate our approach across three models: Llama-2-7B-chat, Llama-2-13B-chat, and Llama-3.2-3B. The 3B checkpoint differs from Llama-2 in family as well as size. Although this comparison does not control variables in the strictest sense, it still shows that the method transfers across model families and across sizes. All experiments were conducted on four RTX 4090 GPUs. The learning rate is 2 × 10 −4. Each task in the stream is trained for two epochs by LoRA with bake training as in Appendix D.2. The geometry loss is extended in multi-layers as in Appendix D.1.

A diverse collection of NLP tasks with expert-written instructions, covering dialogue generation, information extraction, question answering, summarization, and sentiment analysis. 7 CONCLUSION 

We presented ChainLoRA , a replay-free continual merging framework built on chain-updated task-vector geometry. The resulting linear merge consolidates transferable directions while preserving task-private knowledge. During the stream, chain-updated initialization copies the previous carrier forward, and a one-sided orthogonality surrogate limits drift from that carrier effectively. We further imposed effective-rank regularization to control long-horizon parameter growth for parameter efficiency. The cost stays constant as the stream lengthens. After training, the archive is absorbed into a single adapter for inference. Experiments show that ChainLoRA is the strongest replay-free method we compare on the long stream and approaches comparable performance on the short stream. 10 AI USE STATEMENT

A rotation of the basis inside a fixed subspace changes the numerical entries of a carrier even though the subspace itself has not moved. If that basis is not brought to the angle at which orthogonality is measured, carriers can already be orthogonal but look coupled, because only the gauge differs. Forcing the penalty in that mismatched gauge then rotates an already acceptable pair and can itself induce forgetting. The alignment below removes this rotational factor by rotating the later carrier onto the earlier one, so the diagnostic sees only what is left after that gauge is fixed.
